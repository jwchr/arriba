## Built and tested
- Bus Lock local slice (issue #30, `docs/BUS_LOCK.md`): fixed departure/boarding stop, waiting/arrival/boarding/left distinctions, manual no-bus and pass-up reports, actual GPS observation history/age and separate timetable projections, overdue location-unknown state, explicit replacement confirmation, return to a lock from other screens, and foreground screen wake with honest availability status. Reports are in memory for the current visit only; no live backend or phone GPS collection. The invented demo alone exposes GPS/time/replacement simulation controls. Domain (22) and wake-lock (11) unit tests pass.
- Last same-line return (D45): inbound PABT trip card shows the final listed departure for its service day, previous usable option, next listed departure and actual calendar dates. Checks the reverse home stop and exact route ID; 165T is not replaced with 165. Printed holidays and missing calendars produce unconfirmed results. 13 unit tests pass. The printed weekday N19→PABT 165 case returns to W19 at 1:10 AM the following date, preceded by 12:32 AM and followed by the next service day's 5:22 AM. Times remain scheduled, not live guarantees.
- Current verification (2026-11-04, this Mac): `npm run verify` passes: TypeScript, 142 unit tests, build, 31 Playwright flows (Chromium installed locally).
- M1 prototype on invented demo data (`src/data/demo.ts`, DEMO banner): home with ordered saved commutes and one-tap reverse, stop search with wrong-side guard, 7-day all-day timetable, departure detail with front-sign, stop list, Port Authority gate card (usual gate), ticket warning, exact-bus lock (scheduled-only until M3), English/Spanish, light/dark, offline service worker.
- Performance: Intl date formatters are built once per time zone and language (`src/domain/time.ts`); a week of Port Authority departures with stop lists went from 1,169 ms to 117 ms on the Mac.
- Domain logic: GTFS service-day time with DST and after-midnight trips, calendars and holiday exceptions, departures, direction guard, favorites, trip lock state machine, gate parsing/selection/change detection. The original 80 unit tests remain; the Bus Lock, wake and last-return additions bring the suite to 126.
- `scripts/build-gtfs.mjs`: real NJ Transit GTFS zip to `public/data/corridor.json` (corridor routes only); tested on a synthetic mini feed. When that file is present the app uses it instead of the demo. Stop times the feed leaves blank are filled in and listed in `trip.estimated`; the app shows them as `~7:20 AM` with a legend.
- NJ Transit data-terms notices (footer and Settings, English and Spanish): data obtained from NJ Transit and redistributed from Dale's own server; may not be real time. The demo shows only the real-time line, since its data is invented.
- Offline hardening (OFF-01, issue #5): the service worker revalidates the page and schedule, uses its saved copy, marked as saved, when the network takes over 4 seconds, fails or answers with a server error; install saves the page, its assets and the schedule so the app opens offline after the first visit; pages are saved under one address without the query string; the app then says "Showing the schedule saved <date, time>" (or that the time is unknown) even when the browser thinks it is online. Spanish dates read day/month/year. The app shows a loading screen and, if nothing loads in 15 seconds, an error with a retry. The demo loads only with `?demo` or when no dataset is published (404), never as a fallback for a failed load.
- Printed fares (UX-09, issue #7): the trip screen shows the zone count and NJ Transit's printed cash fare (adult; child 5-11 or senior 62+), from the route 165 timetable's fare tables (fares effective 7/1/26) and the four fare zone lines drawn on its map, with the source and date. Zone bands per timepoint are in `data/t0165/transcription.json` (`fares`); GSP Exit 165 Park/Ride (13) and Kinderkamack Rd. at Oradell Ave. (15, to Westwood) are where the map says the zone changes, so trips from or to them show a range ("3 or 4 zones", "$5.45–$7.30") and "ask the driver". Port Authority departures say a ticket or pass is needed instead of the cash note (driver makes change, bills up to $20). Not done: a warning once the printed fares are a year old (NJ Transit changes fares each July 1); M2 replaces them. No fare is shown without a fare table (demo; GTFS until M2 adds one). Not shown: transfers, ticket and app prices.
- First-ride guide (UX-07, issue #9): a screen reached from home and Settings with numbered steps (before you go; paying in New Jersey: cash on route 165's full-service line, Tap & Ride with zones told to the driver, app and paper tickets; leaving from Port Authority: ticket first, no cash or Tap & Ride on board, gate, accessible gate; getting off; help line 973-275-5555), English and Spanish. Every step is from the printed route 165 timetable or njtransit.com "How to Ride - Bus", "Rider Guide for Bus Customers" and /tap (checked 2026-11-03), and the screen says so. NJ Transit's general page says to pay the exact fare in cash; its rider guide says full-service routes make change, and the 165 timetable says it is a full-service line, so the guide says the driver makes change.
- Stop search ranks stops in a town ahead of stops whose "(to ...)" label names it (searching "Westwood" used to cut off the Sand Rd. stop), and "Where are you going?" offers every stop a bus from here reaches (Port Authority to Hackensack works), then wrong-side stops for the guard. Reversing such a commute boards across the street when no bus goes home from the stop itself (`returnLeg`); with no twin and no coordinates it says no return bus is in the schedule.
- Home-screen departures that board at Port Authority show the gate on the row (issue #15), from the same answer as the trip screen and labeled by kind: posted, "Usually gate", "· printed rule", or "Gate not confirmed". At M3 the posted line (and the trip card) must add the posting's age.
- The gate card carries NJ Transit's construction advisory (station advisory 2176177, checked 2026-11-03: gate assignments may change). Re-check it and remove the line when the advisory ends.
- Home-screen install: Chrome's installability check reports no errors. PNG icons for phones (iPhone apple-touch-icon 180 px; Android 192/512 px and a full-bleed maskable 512 px) are rendered from `public/icon.svg` by `node scripts/build-icons.mjs` (rerun after changing the SVG). The guide has a sixth card with home-screen steps for Chrome, Samsung Internet and Safari (general phone steps, said so on screen).
- Accessibility: `tests/e2e/a11y.spec.ts` runs axe-core (WCAG 2.1 A/AA rules) on every screen (home, search, destinations, trip, timetable, Settings, guide, demo, wrong-side warning, edit mode, load error) in light and dark, English and Spanish; the first run found past timetable rows faded below contrast (now the muted color). Test-only dependency: `@axe-core/playwright` 4.14.0 with axe-core 4.14.0, MPL-2.0, pinned exact.
- Original 22 Playwright flows (16 + 6 accessibility runs) on a Pixel 7 viewport; four stub the dataset (NJ Transit source line and estimate marker; failed load and retry; saved copy; no dataset published), and seven run on the committed route 165 data (two check the clock-change notes; one checks departure rows fit a 320 px phone at 130% text in both languages). Six Bus Lock/last-return flows bring the total to 28; browser verification status is above.
- Route 165 printed timetable (owner-supplied PDF "T0165 - 7/26", issued 7/1/26) is typed in as `data/t0165/transcription.json` and built by `scripts/build-timetable.mjs` into the committed `public/data/corridor.json`, so the app now shows real 165/165T times (500 printed trips: weekday, Saturday, Sunday, both directions). The app shows a "typed in from NJ Transit's printed timetable" banner. NJ Transit's printed Port Authority gate rules (gate change effective 3/15/2025) show as "Gate 212, door 1" etc. with a "general rule, not posted for this bus" note. Printed rider notes shown in the app: the Westwood first-pickup note, and the Exit 165 Park/Ride note (PM Parkway express trips stop there only if the rider asks when boarding), shown when the rider gets off there from a P or FP trip. To rebuild: `npm run timetable -- --built-at <ISO>`; `tests/unit/timetable.test.ts` fails if the committed JSON and the transcription disagree.
- Limits of that data: 20 timepoints per direction only (no intermediate stops, so a rider's own stop may be missing); no coordinates, so the wrong-side guard works only through named twin stops (same printed location both ways, 11 pairs; other map references differ by direction and get no twin); no holiday schedules: the PDF lists 25 holidays but not their times, so on those days (`src/domain/holidays.ts`) the app shows the ordinary times with a "may run a different schedule" warning at the top, in the timetable and on the trip; the end date of the schedule is unknown, so calendars run to 2099-12-31 and the banner is the only warning; headsigns are Dale's own "route + last timepoint" labels, not sign text; FP trips and F meaning are read from the legend, which defines F and P but not FP; DST: each printed trip starts at its printed clock time and keeps its printed running times (`tripTimeAt`), so a bus running through a clock change is never shown later than it is. Only the Saturday 1:10 AM Westwood trip runs into the changed hour: on the fall-back night its last two stops (printed 2:03, 2:08 AM) show 1:03 and 1:08 AM (assumption: the bus does not wait out the repeated hour; nothing printed says it does); on the spring-forward night they show 3:03 and 3:08 AM; the trip screen says when a trip runs through the 2 AM change (`clockChangeBetween`, for the rider's own ride), and the all-day timetable says so on that day only when a listed bus runs through the change, English and Spanish. Transcription method: per-cell OCR with AM (light) / PM (bold) classified from stroke weight, resolved by a monotone-time search per row, structural checks (no step over 45 min, every cell valid), four independent passes compared, about 25 cells corrected by eye, and an 18-row random visual check with no errors found. It is not NJ Transit's feed: replace it with `build-gtfs.mjs` output at M2 and compare the two.
